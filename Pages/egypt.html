The Discovery of King Tutankhamun's Tomb

Hidden beneath the sands of Egypt's Valley of the Kings for more than three thousand years, the tomb of King Tutankhamun remained largely untouched until its remarkable discovery in 1922 by British archaeologist Howard Carter. Unlike many royal tombs that had been looted over centuries, Tutankhamun's burial chamber was found almost intact, preserving over 5,000 extraordinary artifacts that offered an unprecedented glimpse into the wealth, craftsmanship, and spiritual beliefs of Ancient Egypt.

Among the treasures were the iconic solid gold funerary mask, elaborate gilded shrines, ceremonial chariots, jewelry, weapons, furniture, and everyday objects carefully placed to accompany the young pharaoh into the afterlife. The discovery transformed modern Egyptology, revealing not only the splendor of New Kingdom royal burials but also the meticulous rituals surrounding death, rebirth, and eternal life in ancient Egyptian civilization.

Timeline of Tutankhamun

Though his reign lasted only about nine years, Tutankhamun played a significant role in restoring Egypt's traditional religion and left behind one of history's greatest archaeological discoveries.

c. 1341 BCE

Birth of Tutankhamun

Born into Egypt's 18th Dynasty, Tutankhamun was likely the son of Pharaoh Akhenaten during the turbulent Amarna Period.

c. 1332 BCE

Ascends the Throne

At approximately nine years old, Tutankhamun became Pharaoh of Egypt, ruling under the guidance of powerful advisors.

Year 1–2

Restoration of Ancient Traditions

The worship of Amun and Egypt's traditional gods was restored. The young king changed his name from Tutankhaten to Tutankhamun, symbolizing the return to the old faith.

Year 3–6

Temple Restoration

Temples and monuments across Egypt were repaired and reopened, reversing many of the religious changes introduced by Akhenaten.

Year 5–8

Diplomacy & Military Campaigns

Egyptian officials restored influence in Nubia and neighboring regions while strengthening trade and diplomatic relations.

c. 1323 BCE

Death of the Boy King

Tutankhamun died unexpectedly at around eighteen or nineteen years of age after ruling Egypt for roughly nine years.

c. 1323 BCE

Burial in the Valley of the Kings

Buried in tomb KV62 alongside more than 5,000 treasures prepared for his eternal journey into the afterlife.

1922 CE

Howard Carter Discovers the Tomb

Nearly 3,200 years after Tutankhamun's death, British archaeologist Howard Carter uncovered the remarkably intact tomb, revealing one of the greatest archaeological discoveries in history.
